Over the past couple of years, I've had the chance to speak at some incredible conferences — GitHub Universe, RSAC, BlackHat, and Microsoft Reactor among them. Each one has taught me something different about communicating complex ideas to diverse audiences.

Here are a few lessons I keep coming back to.

## Know your audience's baseline

At BlackHat, the audience knows what a CVE is. At a general tech conference, they might not. The same talk can land completely differently depending on how much context you provide upfront.

I always try to spend the first few minutes of a talk calibrating. A quick poll, a show of hands, or even just reading the room's reaction to an opening statement can tell you a lot about where to pitch your content.

## Stories beat slides

The talks I remember from other speakers — and the ones that get the best feedback when I give them — are built around stories, not bullet points. 

A real example of a supply chain attack that affected real developers is infinitely more compelling than a chart showing "vulnerabilities are increasing." People remember narratives. They forget statistics.

## Nerves never fully go away

I've given dozens of talks at this point, and I still get nervous. The trick I've learned is that nervousness and excitement feel almost identical in your body. I just choose to interpret the butterflies as excitement.

## The hallway track is the real conference

The best conversations I've had at conferences happen between sessions — in the hallway, at lunch, or at the evening events. If you're attending a conference, don't just sit in talks all day. Make time to meet people.

If you're thinking about submitting a CFP for the first time: do it. The community is more welcoming than you think, and your unique perspective is exactly what makes a talk worth attending.
