People are always surprised when I tell them I studied philosophy in college. "How did you end up in tech?" they ask, as if reading Kant and shipping software are worlds apart.

But the truth is, philosophy gave me the single most important skill I use every day as a PM: the ability to ask *why* relentlessly until I reach something true.

## The Socratic method is just user research

Socrates wandered around Athens asking people to define their terms. "What is justice? What is courage? What do you *actually* mean when you say that?"

That's... basically what product discovery is. You talk to users, and you keep pushing past the surface-level feature requests until you understand the real problem. "I need a dashboard" usually means "I can't find the information I need quickly." Those are very different problems with very different solutions.

## Logic teaches you to spot bad arguments

Philosophy trains you to identify logical fallacies, and product management is full of them:

- **Appeal to authority:** "The VP wants this feature, so we should build it."
- **Sunk cost fallacy:** "We've already spent three months on this, we can't stop now."
- **False dichotomy:** "We either build this now or we lose the customer."

Being able to name these patterns — and gently redirect the conversation — is an underrated PM superpower.

## Ethics matters in product

We're building tools that millions of people use. The questions philosophy taught me to ask — *Who benefits? Who is harmed? What are the second-order effects?* — come up constantly in product decisions.

Should we optimize for engagement or well-being? Should we make this data available or protect privacy? These aren't just business questions. They're ethical ones.

## The bottom line

If you're a philosophy major wondering whether tech is right for you: it is. Your training in critical thinking, structured argumentation, and asking uncomfortable questions is exactly what this industry needs more of.

Still asking "why?" after all these years. Wouldn't have it any other way.
